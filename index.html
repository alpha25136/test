Inservice Agenda & Core Curriculum

1. Lead Placement & Signal Quality

The 5-Lead Configuration (Standard):

  • White: Right arm (Infraclavicular fossa, near right shoulder).
  • Black: Left arm (Infraclavicular fossa, near left shoulder).
  • Brown: Chest/V1 position (4th intercostal space, right sternal border).
  • Green: Right leg (Lower right abdomen/rib border).
  • Red: Left leg (Lower left abdomen/rib border).

Skin Prep Protocol:

Shave excess hair, cleanse skin thoroughly with alcohol or soap/water, and ensure skin dries completely to minimize artifact impedance.

Troubleshooting:

Recognize muscle tremors vs. loose electrodes vs. respiratory artifact.

2. Lethal Arrhythmias & Immediate Actions

Ventricular Fibrillation (V-Fib) & Pulseless V-Tach:

  • Check the patient first (verify pulselessness/responsiveness).
  • Call a Code Blue. Start high-quality CPR immediately. Defibrillate ASAP.

Asystole / PEA:

  • Confirm in two leads. Check responsiveness, pulse, and line connections.
  • Call a Code Blue. Start CPR. Administer Epinephrine per ACLS guidelines (Do not shock).

Third-Degree (Complete) AV Block:

  • Assess hemodynamic stability (BP, mental status).
  • Prepare transcutaneous pacing (TCP) pads and notify the provider immediately.

3. Documentation & Alarm Fatigue Management

Strip Analysis:

Analyze and document rhythm strips every 4 to 12 hours (per hospital policy). Measure PR, QRS, and QTc intervals.

Alarm Customization:

Adjust alarm parameters to match the patient's baseline (e.g., set parameters to 50-120 bpm for a patient with controlled atrial fibrillation) to mitigate alarm fatigue.

Never pause or disable lethal alarms.
